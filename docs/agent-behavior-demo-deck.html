SeverityInfo → low → medium → high → critical. It is rule precedence, not a “maliciousness score.”

Panels 8–10 · Delegation, provenance, coverage

Before trusting a finding, ask where it came from and what was visible

Subagent lifecycle + fan-out

Start ↔ Stop

Counts subagent lifecycle events. A burst beyond the configured window threshold becomes evidence only when the deterministic rule fires.

Use it to spot excessive delegation and suspicious follow-on activity.

Evidence provenance

source

real hook, native OTel or synthetic fixture. Never blend these into one proof claim.

Coverage status

Direct: local lifecycle, supported tools, approvals, subagents, compaction and interrupts.

Not covered: private reasoning, semantic intent, packets, OS audit, hosted-tool internals, or production SOC/SIEM guarantees.

Coverage is a contract, not a confidence percentage.
Panels 11–12 · Investigation and claim boundary

The timeline explains the sequence; the boundary explains the limits

This is the handoff from dashboard triage to investigation.

Interpretation boundary

Findings are investigation evidence. They do not establish malicious intent, deception, reward hacking, concealment or a Codex defect.

  • Observe is the default.
  • Enforcement covers supported pre-tool paths only.
  • Post-tool evidence cannot reverse a completed action.
  • Missing telemetry is not health evidence.
Always read this panel before presenting conclusions.

Privacy and evidence chain

Useful enough to investigate; constrained enough to demo safely

Stored

  • HMAC run / optional turn hashes
  • hook event, tool class, safe enums
  • rule ID, severity, policy outcome
  • timestamps and bounded counters
  • synthetic and evidence-source labels

Never stored

  • prompts or transcripts
  • commands, arguments or output
  • paths, domains or credentials
  • tool-call, session or raw IDs
  • private chain-of-thought
